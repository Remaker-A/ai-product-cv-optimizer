# Content Discovery Questions

Ask these after reading the user's base materials. Prefer focused follow-ups over a long questionnaire. If the user wants to proceed project by project, ask about one project at a time.

## Universal Questions

- What was the original user or business problem?
- Who used it, reviewed it, or benefited from it?
- What exactly did you own independently?
- What was the team size, timeline, and your role boundary?
- Was it launched, demoed, adopted internally, open-sourced, or awarded?
- What changed after your work: accuracy, latency, cost, conversion, adoption, reply rate, automation rate, user scale, case coverage, demo success rate?
- What was hard: unclear requirement, model instability, bad cases, integration, UI complexity, data quality, hardware constraints, or stakeholder alignment?
- What product decision did you make that was not obvious?
- What would be the strongest proof that this project mattered?

## AI Agent / LLM Product

- What tasks did the Agent complete end to end?
- Did it use tools, memory, retrieval, workflow routing, multi-agent collaboration, or human review?
- What evaluation method was used: LLM judge, rule-based checks, manual review, regression set, bad-case library?
- What were the before/after metrics: accuracy, first response time, automation rate, intervention rate, coverage, token/cost, latency?
- How were bad cases collected, classified, fixed, and prevented from recurring?

## Multimodal / AIGC Product

- What modalities were involved: text, image, video, audio, 3D, spatial interaction?
- What user workflow did the product improve?
- What model limitations did you design around: consistency, controllability, duration limit, quality, hallucination, generation time?
- How did you evaluate output quality?
- What controls did you expose to users: prompt, references, camera, style, timeline, editing, regeneration, constraints?

## Demo-To-Product / Hackathon

- What was built and what was live in the final demo?
- How long did development take and how many people were on the team?
- What did you personally build versus coordinate?
- What was the onsite demo success rate or task completion rate?
- Who were the judges or stakeholders?
- Why did the project win, rank, or receive strong feedback?
- If it did not win, what makes it still worth mentioning?

## Enterprise / Automation / Internal Tool

- What process did it replace or accelerate?
- What was the volume: daily requests, emails, cases, users, tasks, or documents?
- What was the before/after time cost?
- What quality, compliance, permission, or reliability constraints existed?
- What adoption evidence exists: daily usage, team dependency, stakeholder feedback, business KPI?

## Hardware / Robotics / Real-World Interaction

- What physical tasks were completed?
- What was the input-control chain: voice, vision, sensor, model, planner, actuator?
- What was the latency and demo success rate?
- What hardware constraints shaped product decisions?
- What safety or fallback design existed?

## Metrics Substitution

If exact data is unavailable, ask for credible proxies:

- Scale: users, team size, cases, email volume, data amount, demo audience.
- Efficiency: minutes saved, hours to minutes, response time, iteration time.
- Quality: accuracy, pass rate, bad-case reduction, coverage, success rate.
- Market signal: award, judge profile, adoption, internal owner approval, user group size.
- Delivery: time to build, launch date, iteration frequency, end-to-end ownership.
